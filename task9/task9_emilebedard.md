# Task9: Project 2 feedback!
by Emile Bedard for CART 263 - april 2026

### Project1 - [Xueyi Xia and Weini Wang: Interactive Web Project](https://xiaxueyi00-eng.github.io/cart263-Project2/Xueyi-Xia/html/index.html)

The interactive web project by Xueyi Xia and Weini Wang really surprised me. I was not sure to fully understand nor comprehend the full broad message that the artists wanted to achieve but I witness the scale and complexity of the task that they decided to tackle. At first impression, I explored and wandered curiously through the web map and tried the different interactions and games to discover the messages and intentions. Overall, I would say that I was left confused and unsatisfied. I feel like the project was too ambitious and that the experience quality paid the price. For example, on third page: SEEN, a bug systematically appears after the initial countdown finishes... I feel like it is a linking issue, but this small mistake has a high impact on the overall experience. Same for the second page: SHARED. The experience is still under construction or unfinished because everything the artist says its supposed to do in the readme is not happening on the page itself... In fact, after digging a bit, console errors are overflowing stating: 

*shared.js:209 Uncaught ReferenceError: centerX is not defined*

Unfortunately, even if the code high ambition of combining many libraries like phaser and Three.js was a great challenge, it was poorly executed and the experience feels unusable.

### Project2 - [Diana Edvi: Procedural Terrain Generator](https://dianaedvi.github.io/CART-263/projects/project_2/)

Diana's experience is fun and efficient. I clearly understood what she built as soon as I saw her work. It is simple yet features advanced techniques, this combination makes her project compelling and complete to me appreciation. I love how she treated immersivity and atmosphere in her Three.js scene, the procedural colors based on altitude, the shimmery lake and the animated clouds all worked together cohesively to deliver a rich experience. After checking through her code, I love to understand the challenges she had to come across and how she built the scene entirely inside three.js. The Perlin noise add-on was a new concept for me, and I love how she integrated it in the project, the pattern it produces really resembles mountains and terrains and every time I tap spacebar to regenerate a terrain, it is convincing and appealing. To look this simple and efficient, I must assume it is a very tricky balance between control and computing freedom. Overall, I love Diana's project and code implementation, and I would be curious to see how she could develop her concept further: Weather simulations? mini cities procedurally integrated? manual lighting system for the user? I think many opportunities are possible here.

### Project3 - [Yejin (Aurora) Oh and Nancy He: Culture Vulture Museum and gift shop](https://yejiweji.github.io/CART263_Project2_-FINAL-/museum/index.html)

Aurora's and Nancy's project was rich and developed. It merged political activism with artistic discourse and history gamification. I really liked how they combined all of these important subjects into one complete and immersive experience. The museum was interesting, I was first curious about the misalignment of elements and questioned if it was intentional before understanding that it was not, the code and implementation was simple not made responsive unfortunately. This major error made the experience exceedingly difficult to appreciate, and it was only in the process video that i understood how it was supposed to look. Concerning the gift shop, I really liked how they continued their message about cultural commodity and consumerism into a small game with reflective questions. My only not would be that I felt a strong dissonance between the style and visual aspect of the museum compared to the visual attributes of the arcade-like gift shop. With finer adjustments and responsive layout fixes, the culture vulture museum and gift shop is a very compelling project.
