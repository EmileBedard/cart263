# Task9: Project 2 feedback!
by Emile Bedard for CART 263 - april 2026

### Project1 - [Philippe Beauchemin: Aquarium](https://philippe-bn.github.io/cart263/projects/project-1/)

Philippe’s project was very interesting to me. His minimalistic approach made me question the complexity of his work but after reading his artist statement and understanding his, I understood the major part of his work was in the research and implementation. The boid’s dynamics and logic felt very smooth, fun and intuitive and the feeling of leadership with the mouse and isolation when we don’t move is very strong. After knowing all of this, I love his project and really appreciate its minimalism. Looking at the code, Aquarium is interesting because it is not 100% created by Philippe. He used the autonomous agents code by author of “the nature of code”: Daniel Shiffman. This meant that his “fishes” are responding and considering each other while still following the mouse coordinates. This context of environment awareness and user interaction makes the experience feel more real and tangible. When we see them colliding after a pause of movement, we can almost feel a moment of empathy for the fish entities.

### Project2 - [Sean Gregory: LIGHTSCRIPT](https://seangregoryv8.github.io/cart263/projects/1/)

Lightscript is a Javascript project with a radical approach. The learning curve to understand and interact with the experiment is steep, but after some careless typing and a few minutes, you can understand the general idea. He questions interface and user agency very well here. While offering no support or care he lets the user battle for air while trying to understand what he is supposed to be doing here. After reading his artist statement and understanding his original idea, this minimalistic and unhelpful approach makes more sense to me. His code is all packed in one file which is interesting to me, I thought that he would have split it up into more compartments. I like how his code is very object oriented and is flexible to any possible action from the user. When he coded his sandbox, it must really have been a challenge to navigate this kind of possibilities from the users. I also like the classic esthetics Sean used here; green and black console, black viewport and classic simple sans-serif font. These design choices made the experience feel calm and classic. We could take the time to learn the interface without feeling rushed and discover many possibilities with little help from the command “help” in the integrated console.

### Project3 - [Benjamin Macknofsky: Worm Fishing Survival](https://benmsky.github.io/CART263-main/CART263-main/project1/)

Ben’s project is a great game. His intentions and inspirations are clear: make a great browser base, wave defense game with the theme of an underwater worm surviving. It’s surprising to me how effective his game is and just how many possibilities there are with the combination of javascript and HTML5! I liked the challenge of the game! It was not easy and I tried to replay a few times because I was enjoying it and I wanted to reach more waves of enemies. His addition of the “easter egg: type worm to get off the hook” is a great idea too! I love the game and the feeling associated completely changed when I tried the secret word. It changes strategically and the user is left to navigate with these new controls. Well done!
